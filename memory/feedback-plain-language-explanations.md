On 4 September 2026 Bryan stopped a detailed nutrition answer and said: *"Can you explain it
as if I don't know what a word you're saying... give me something easy to read. This is hard
for me to read to understand you."*

The answer that failed was dense with technical shorthand — BMI, BMR, TDEE, Mifflin-St Jeor,
p-values, multi-column tables — stacked without unpacking. The rewrite that worked used
short sentences, one idea per line, plain words, and numbers stated in context ("you're about
900 calories short every day") rather than as equations.

**Why:** He reads English fluently but it is not his first language, and unexplained
technical vocabulary makes an otherwise correct answer useless to him. This is the same
pattern as [[user-bryan-profile]]: he will not ask what a term means, so an unexplained term
just silently costs him the point.

**How to apply:** Default to plain language outside his own domains. Spell out any acronym
the first time or drop it entirely. Prefer short paragraphs and numbered steps over tables
of jargon; keep tables for simple like-for-like comparisons. Give the conclusion in words
before the arithmetic. This does not mean shorter or less rigorous — the research and the
real figures still go in, they just get said plainly. It also does not apply to his
professional documents in Bahasa Indonesia, where the technical register is the deliverable.
